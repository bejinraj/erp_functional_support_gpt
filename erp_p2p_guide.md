# ERP Process Guide - Procure-to-Pay (P2P) Module

## Overview of Procure-to-Pay

Procure-to-Pay (P2P) covers the complete procurement cycle, from requesting goods or services to paying the supplier.

Process Flow: Purchase Requisition → PR Approval → Purchase Order → Goods Receipt → Invoice Verification → Invoice Posting → Payment Processing.

## Process 1: Creating a Purchase Requisition (PR)

Role: Requester / Department Employee

Purpose: To formally request goods or services before a Purchase Order can be created.

Steps:

1. Navigate to Materials Management → Purchase Requisition → Create.
2. Enter the material or service description, quantity, and required delivery date.
3. Select the appropriate plant, storage location, and purchasing group, as applicable.
4. Enter the estimated price and account assignment, such as cost center or GL account, if required.
5. Attach supporting documents or specifications, if applicable.
6. Review all entered details for accuracy.
7. Submit the Purchase Requisition for approval.
8. Note the PR number for future reference.

Expected Result: The Purchase Requisition is created and submitted for approval.

## Process 2: Approving a Purchase Requisition

Role: Department Manager / Budget Approver

Purpose: To verify that the purchase is necessary, budgeted, and authorized.

Steps:

1. Navigate to the Purchase Requisition Approval section.
2. Search for the PR using the requisition number or approval worklist.
3. Review the material or service description, quantity, estimated cost, and delivery date.
4. Verify the budget and account assignment.
5. Check the supporting documents and business justification.
6. Approve the PR if all details are correct.
7. Reject or return the PR for correction if any information is missing or incorrect.
8. Confirm that the PR status is updated to Approved or Released.

Expected Result: The Purchase Requisition is approved and ready for purchasing.

## Process 3: Creating a Purchase Order (PO)

Role: Procurement Executive / Buyer

Purpose: To create an official order for the approved goods or services from a supplier.

Steps:

1. Navigate to Materials Management → Purchasing → Purchase Order → Create.
2. Select the appropriate PO document type.
3. Enter the approved PR number as a reference, where applicable.
4. Enter or select the supplier/vendor details.
5. Verify the material or service, quantity, price, delivery date, and delivery location.
6. Enter payment terms, currency, tax details, and other applicable conditions.
7. Verify the purchasing organization, purchasing group, and company code.
8. Review the PO for accuracy and submit it for approval.
9. After approval, issue the PO to the supplier.
10. Record the PO number for tracking.

Expected Result: The Purchase Order is created, approved, and communicated to the supplier.

## Process 4: Approving and Releasing a Purchase Order

Role: Procurement Manager / Authorized Approver

Purpose: To ensure that the Purchase Order complies with company purchasing policies before it is issued.

Steps:

1. Navigate to the Purchase Order Approval or Release section.
2. Search for the PO using its document number.
3. Review supplier details, order value, quantities, prices, and delivery terms.
4. Compare the PO with the approved PR, quotation, or contract.
5. Verify compliance with the applicable approval limits.
6. Approve or release the PO if all details are correct.
7. Return the PO for correction if discrepancies are identified.
8. Confirm that the PO has reached the approved or released status.

Expected Result: The Purchase Order is authorized for issuance to the supplier.

## Process 5: Recording Goods Receipt (GR)

Role: Warehouse Executive / Goods Receiving Team

Purpose: To confirm that the goods ordered through the Purchase Order have been received.

Steps:

1. Navigate to Materials Management → Inventory Management → Goods Receipt.
2. Select the Goods Receipt option with reference to a Purchase Order.
3. Enter the PO number to retrieve the order details.
4. Verify the delivered material, quantity, and unit of measure against the PO.
5. Inspect the goods for damage, quality issues, or shortages.
6. Enter the actual received quantity and storage location, where applicable.
7. Record batch or serial numbers if required.
8. Post the Goods Receipt after validating the details.
9. Note the Goods Receipt document number.
10. Report any discrepancies to the procurement team.

Expected Result: The Goods Receipt is posted, and inventory records are updated as applicable.

## Process 6: Recording Service Entry

Role: Service Owner / Service Entry Approver

Purpose: To confirm that the supplier has completed the contracted services.

Steps:

1. Navigate to the Service Entry section in the ERP system.
2. Enter the relevant Purchase Order number.
3. Review the ordered service description and agreed value.
4. Verify the service completion details with the responsible department.
5. Enter the quantity or service value completed.
6. Attach service completion reports or supporting documents, if required.
7. Submit the service entry for approval.
8. Obtain approval from the authorized service owner.
9. Confirm that the service entry is accepted in the ERP system.

Expected Result: The completed services are recorded and approved for invoice processing.

## Process 7: Supplier Invoice Verification

Role: Accounts Payable (AP) Executive

Purpose: To verify the supplier invoice against the Purchase Order and Goods Receipt or Service Entry before posting.

Steps:

1. Navigate to Logistics Invoice Verification or Supplier Invoice Processing.
2. Receive and review the supplier invoice.
3. Enter the supplier invoice number, invoice date, and invoice amount.
4. Enter the relevant PO number.
5. Verify the supplier details, quantity, unit price, tax, freight, and payment terms.
6. Compare the invoice with the PO and Goods Receipt or approved Service Entry.
7. Check for duplicate invoices and discrepancies.
8. Resolve any price or quantity mismatch with the procurement or receiving team.
9. Hold or block the invoice if discrepancies exceed approved tolerances.
10. Confirm that the invoice passes all required validation checks.

Expected Result: The supplier invoice is verified and ready for posting or further approval.

## Process 8: Posting a Supplier Invoice

Role: Accounts Payable Executive / Finance Executive

Purpose: To record the supplier invoice in the ERP system and recognize the amount payable.

Steps:

1. Navigate to Finance → Accounts Payable → Supplier Invoice Posting.
2. Enter the supplier invoice details and relevant PO reference.
3. Verify the company code, supplier account, currency, and invoice date.
4. Validate the tax codes, payment terms, and accounting details.
5. Confirm that the invoice has passed the required matching and approval checks.
6. Review the invoice amount and applicable accounting entries.
7. Post the invoice in the ERP system.
8. Record the invoice document number.
9. Check the invoice posting status and due date.
10. Follow up on any blocked, parked, or rejected invoices.

Expected Result: The invoice is posted, and the supplier liability is recorded in the financial system.

## Process 9: Processing Supplier Payments

Role: Accounts Payable Executive / Treasury Team

Purpose: To pay approved supplier invoices according to agreed payment terms.

Steps:

1. Navigate to Finance → Accounts Payable → Payment Processing.
2. Identify invoices that are due for payment.
3. Review invoice status, due dates, payment blocks, and credit notes.
4. Verify the supplier's approved bank details.
5. Select eligible invoices for the payment run.
6. Generate the payment proposal or payment batch.
7. Review the proposed payments for accuracy and completeness.
8. Obtain the required payment authorization.
9. Execute the payment through the approved payment process.
10. Record the payment reference and verify the payment status.
11. Send remittance advice to the supplier, where applicable.

Expected Result: The approved supplier invoices are paid, and the payment status is updated in the ERP system.

## Process 10: Supplier Account Reconciliation

Role: Accounts Payable Executive / Finance Analyst

Purpose: To ensure that supplier balances and payment records are accurate and complete.

Steps:

1. Navigate to Finance → Accounts Payable → Supplier Account Reports.
2. Select the supplier account and relevant reporting period.
3. Review open invoices, cleared invoices, credit notes, and outstanding payments.
4. Compare the ERP supplier statement with the supplier's statement.
5. Identify missing invoices, duplicate postings, payment differences, or unapplied credits.
6. Investigate and resolve discrepancies with the relevant teams.
7. Verify that payments have been correctly cleared against invoices.
8. Review outstanding Goods Receipt/Invoice Receipt (GR/IR) balances where applicable.
9. Document unresolved items and assign follow-up actions.
10. Complete reconciliation according to the company's closing procedures.

Expected Result: Supplier accounts are reconciled, outstanding items are identified, and financial records are accurate.

## Process 11: Handling Invoice Discrepancies

Role: Accounts Payable Executive / Procurement Executive

Purpose: To investigate and resolve invoice mismatches before payment.

Steps:

1. Identify the invoice that is blocked or has failed validation.
2. Review the invoice, PO, and Goods Receipt or Service Entry.
3. Identify the discrepancy, such as a price difference, quantity mismatch, missing receipt, or incorrect tax.
4. Contact the procurement, receiving, or supplier team as required.
5. Obtain corrected documentation or the necessary authorization.
6. Update the relevant ERP records if corrections are approved.
7. Reprocess the invoice through the applicable validation workflow.
8. Confirm that the invoice is approved for posting or payment.
9. Document the resolution for audit and tracking purposes.

Expected Result: The invoice discrepancy is resolved, and the invoice can proceed through the P2P process.

## Process 12: Monitoring P2P Transactions and Reports

Role: P2P Process Executive / Procurement Analyst

Purpose: To monitor transaction progress, identify pending items, and ensure that activities are completed within defined timelines.

Steps:

1. Navigate to the relevant ERP procurement and accounts payable reports.
2. Review pending PRs and POs awaiting approval.
3. Identify overdue deliveries and outstanding Goods Receipts.
4. Monitor blocked invoices and invoices awaiting posting.
5. Review invoices approaching or exceeding payment due dates.
6. Identify workflow errors and transactions exceeding the expected completion time.
7. Follow up with the responsible teams to resolve pending items.
8. Update trackers or process-query tickets, where applicable.
9. Escalate SLA breaches or critical issues to the appropriate team.
10. Prepare periodic reports on pending transactions and process performance.

Expected Result: P2P transactions are monitored effectively, exceptions are followed up, and processing delays are reduced.

## 13. Important ERP Terms

| Term                   | Meaning                                          |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| PR                     | Purchase Requisition                             |
| PO                     | Purchase Order                                   |
| GR                     | Goods Receipt                                    |
| SES                    | Service Entry Sheet                              |
| GR/IR                  | Goods Receipt / Invoice Receipt clearing account |
| AP                     | Accounts Payable                                 |
| Vendor/Supplier Master | Records containing supplier information          |
| Three-Way Match        | Comparison of PO, receipt, and invoice           |
| Payment Terms          | Conditions defining when payment is due          |
| SLA                    | Service Level Agreement                          |

Note: Menu paths and transaction codes vary depending on the ERP system and configuration. In SAP, common examples include ME51N for PR creation, ME21N for PO creation, MIGO for goods receipt, MIRO for logistics invoice verification, and F110 for automatic payments. Always follow your organization's approved process and authorization rules.
